About

Biography & experience

स्तरों के नीचे स्मृति

Tracing Stories, Reimagining Archives, Connecting Humanities and Technology

Dr. Reema Chowdhary is the Centre Administrator at the JPN National Centre of Excellence in the Humanities, Indian Institute of Technology Indore. Her interdisciplinary work spans Digital Humanities, film studies, cultural heritage, multilingual publishing, and cultural memory, exploring how narratives are represented, preserved, and reinterpreted through digital technologies.

She holds a PhD in English and Film Studies from IIT Indore, where her research examined Indian biopics as historical palimpsests, investigating how cinema reshapes historical narratives and collective memory. Her current research explores digital archives, multilingual knowledge systems, and the preservation of marginalised literary traditions, including Sindhi literature and folklore. Through initiatives such as Sindhi Sanchaya, she contributes to making dispersed cultural heritage more accessible.

Her scholarly work has appeared in journals including the Journal of Electronic Publishing, Archival Science, Preservation, Digital Technology & Culture, South Asian Popular Culture, and Quarterly Review of Film and Video. Her book, Indian Biopics as Historical Palimpsest, is forthcoming with Routledge India. She is Associate Editor of Digital Humanities Intersections and a reviewer for the Journal of Cultural Analytics.

A recipient of the British Council Charles Wallace India Trust Fellowship, she was a Visiting Research Fellow at Nottingham Trent University, UK, in 2017. Her experience spans teaching, research, publishing, and academic administration.

At JPN Centre, IIT Indore, she works at the intersection of research development, digital scholarship, and cultural heritage initiatives. Across her work, she explores a central question: How can we preserve not just cultural materials, but also the histories, voices, and relationships that give them meaning?

Research

Research focus

चार सूत्र, एक ताना-बाना

I

Visual palimpsest

Biopics such as Bandit Queen and Jodhaa Akbar are read as nationalist palimpsests — images that overwrite, yet still leak, older social hegemonies.

II

Muslim identity on screen

Representation in Deepa Mehta’s Earth and Abhishek Kapoor’s Kai Po Che — how popular film carries minority memory after Partition and Gujarat 2002.

III

Multilingual scholarship

India’s scheduled languages cannot live in a monolingual academy. KSHIP, OCR, and open infrastructures become acts of epistemic justice.

IV

Folklore as living archive

Sindhi libraries after Partition and heritage-site sentiment — folklore not as relic, but as a language still trying to belong.
